# Upwork sales hiring — the brief, the mechanics, and the answers

Two audiences. **Part 1** is context to paste into a fresh Claude (Chrome or
otherwise) so it can help set things up and answer candidates. **Part 2** is
the question-and-answer sheet — use it yourself, or hand it over.

---

# PART 1 — Paste this into Claude

I run **FieldQuo**, software that field-service contractors — painters,
plumbers, electricians, roofers, landscapers — pay a monthly or annual
subscription to use. I am hiring **sales closers through Upwork** to sign
those businesses up. They are contractors, not employees, and they are paid
only for results.

## The commission — this shapes everything else

A rep earns **$125 CAD per customer they sign, but never in one payment.** It
arrives in three stages, each triggered by something the *customer* does:

| Stage | Amount (CAD) | Triggered when | Roughly |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Activation | **$20** | the business finishes payment setup | day 0 |
| 2 — First billing cycle | **$40** | their first real subscription payment succeeds | ~day 30 |
| 3 — Retention | **$65** | still subscribed 60 days after signing up | day 60 |

Three consequences:

1. **I cannot know in advance what a rep will earn or when.** It depends on
   how their customers behave, not on the rep finishing a task.
2. **One sale pays out across about two months.** A rep's first month
   understates their run rate — two thirds of everything sold is still in
   transit.
3. **A customer can refund or cancel after I have already paid.** Upwork has
   no clawback.

My own system already tracks every stage, keyed so a payment event replayed
twice cannot pay twice, and closes **weekly batches** — one total per rep per
week, itemised by customer and stage.

## How I want to pay: weekly milestones on one contract

**One fixed-price contract per rep**, opened once and left open. Each week I
**add one milestone** covering everything that rep earned that week, fund it,
and release it. For example:

> *Week of 8 September — 5 activations, 3 first cycles, 2 retentions — $350 CAD*

I chose this over the two alternatives deliberately:

- **Not hourly** — I am paying for outcomes, not time.
- **Not a milestone per customer** — three stages per customer means a rep
  closing ten a month generates thirty milestones, and any customer who
  churns before day 60 leaves an orphan milestone that can never be
  completed. Weekly batching means a milestone is only ever created for money
  that is already owed, so nothing is ever left unfinished.
- **Not bonuses** — bonuses need no escrow, which sounds convenient until you
  realise escrow is the thing that reassures a good closer. I would rather
  fund it and let them see it.

## What I need help with

1. **Verify my understanding of Upwork's mechanics before I commit**, and
   correct me where I am wrong. Specifically:
   - Can I add new milestones to an **active** fixed-price contract, without
     renegotiating or ending it? Is there a limit on how many?
   - Can I fund and release several reps' milestones in one go, or is it one
     at a time? This decides whether twenty reps a week is fine or painful.
   - What exactly does the freelancer see between funding and release?
   - Confirm the security hold — my understanding is released funds sit
     "pending" for about five days before they can be withdrawn.
2. **What Upwork's fee is and who pays it**, so I can tell a rep what they
   actually receive on $125 CAD. I will not let anyone discover that on their
   first payout.
3. **Whether the contract can be denominated in CAD**, or whether Upwork
   converts. My reps need to know which currency they are being quoted.
4. **Whether there is any way to reverse a released payment.** If there is
   not, say so plainly — I will handle refunds by netting against the
   following week.

**How to work:** read and report before you act. Stop before anything that
spends money, creates a contract, or sends an offer — show me the amounts and
the recipient and wait. Never enter payment details or passwords; tell me and
I will do that part. If Upwork works differently from what I have described,
tell me — I would rather change the plan than force it.

# PART 2 — The questions candidates ask, and the answers

Good closers ask these. Answering them straight is what keeps the good ones.

**"How many paying customers do you have?"**
Answer honestly. If the number is small or zero, say so and say what *is*
true — the product is live, the lead list is real, the territory is open. A
closer who finds out in week two has lost trust in you, and they were right
to. Do not invent a number.

**"How many closers are on the list already?"**
Say the real figure. Early is an advantage here: territory density is not a
constraint, and leads that are claimed but not worked return to the pool
after 48 hours, so nothing sits reserved by someone who is not calling.

**"How do the milestones work if this is ongoing commission?"**
One contract, one milestone per week, itemised by stage. See Part 1. The key
sentence: *"a milestone is only ever created for money you have already
earned, so there is never an incomplete one."*

**"What if a stage never happens?"**
Then no milestone is created for it. If a customer cancels before day 60,
stage 3 does not exist — it is not an unpaid milestone, it never becomes one.

**"So is it $125 a week per sale?"**
No, and this is the one to be clearest about. **$125 is what one customer
pays out in total, over about two months.** In any given week a rep is paid
for a mix: activations from that week, first cycles from customers signed a
month earlier, retentions from two months earlier. Week one of a rep's first
month is $20 per activation and nothing else.

**"Is your payment method verified?"**
Verify it before you post the role. To a freelancer, an unverified payment
method reads as "this might not be real", and it is the cheapest objection
you will ever remove.

**"What currency?"**
CAD. Say it in the job post, not in week three.

**"What happens if a customer refunds after I have been paid?"**
Tell them the truth: it nets against the following week, it is rare, and they
will always be shown which customer and why. If they have no following week,
that is a cost you absorb — decide that before it happens, not during.

## Two things to decide before you post the role

**A guarantee for the first cohort.** Commission-only against a product with
few or no customers selects for people who have no better option. A closer
with 30,000 outbound minutes has better options and will price the risk. A
four to six week guarantee, recoverable against commission, costs money
before revenue — but hiring cheap reps who cannot sell an unproven product
costs more and takes longer to discover.

**Whether the fee comes out of their $125 or yours.** Either answer is fine.
Not deciding is not.
